# Mathematics for Machine Learning: Day-by-Day Roadmap

## Beginner to Advanced Foundations

**Duration:** 120 study days (about 4 months)\
**Suggested pace:** 2--3 hours per day, 6 days per week, with the
seventh day used for review or rest. If you have more time, deepen the
exercises rather than rushing ahead.

This roadmap covers **mathematics only**. It is designed to build the
mathematical foundations needed to understand classical machine
learning, deep learning, and modern AI. It starts with arithmetic and
algebra and progresses through calculus, probability, statistics, linear
algebra, optimization, and selected advanced topics.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## How to use this plan

For each study day:

1.  **Understand:** Read or watch an explanation of the topic.
2.  **Work by hand:** Solve the written exercises without a calculator
    where practical.
3.  **Verify:** Use Python only after attempting the problems yourself.
    Python is a checking tool here, not a replacement for mathematical
    reasoning.
4.  **Explain:** Write a short explanation in your own words, including
    one example.
5.  **Review:** Revisit mistakes the next day.

### Daily study structure

  Activity                          Time
  ------------------------- ------------
  Learn the concept           35--45 min
  Worked examples             25--35 min
  Solve exercises by hand     45--60 min
  Review and write notes      15--20 min

### Mastery rule

Do not move on just because you have watched a lesson. Move on when you
can: - explain the concept without looking at notes; - solve a few
standard problems independently; - identify common mistakes; - connect
the concept to a later ML idea when relevant.

# Part 1 --- Number sense and arithmetic (Days 1--10)

**Goal:** Become comfortable manipulating numbers, fractions, signs,
powers, roots, and ratios. These operations appear constantly in
formulas, loss functions, probability, and optimization.

## Day 1 --- Number systems and the number line

-   Learn natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, rational numbers,
    irrational numbers, and real numbers.
-   Understand how these sets relate: natural ⊂ whole ⊂ integers ⊂
    rationals ⊂ reals.
-   Place positive numbers, negative numbers, fractions, and decimals on
    a number line.
-   Understand zero as the origin and the difference between a number's
    value and its position.
-   Practice: classify 12 numbers; order mixed positive and negative
    values; convert simple fractions to decimals.
-   **You should be able to:** explain what makes a number rational or
    irrational and locate it on a number line.

## Day 2 --- Addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division

-   Review the four arithmetic operations.
-   Learn operation order: brackets, powers, multiplication/division,
    addition/subtraction.
-   Practise negative-number rules:
    -   positive × positive = positive;
    -   negative × negative = positive;
    -   positive × negative = negative.
-   Understand why division by zero is undefined.
-   Practice expressions containing brackets and negative numbers.
-   **You should be able to:** simplify a multi-step arithmetic
    expression correctly.

## Day 3 --- Fractions and equivalent values

-   Understand numerator and denominator.
-   Generate equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing numerator
    and denominator by the same nonzero number.
-   Simplify fractions using common factors and the greatest common
    divisor (GCD).
-   Add and subtract fractions using a common denominator.
-   Multiply and divide fractions.
-   Practice: simplify 18/24; calculate 2/3 + 3/4; calculate (5/6) ÷
    (2/3).
-   **You should be able to:** perform all four operations with
    fractions and explain why equivalent fractions have the same value.

## Day 4 --- Decimals, percentages, and conversions

-   Convert fractions to decimals and percentages.
-   Convert percentages to fractions and decimals.
-   Calculate a percentage of a quantity.
-   Understand percentage increase and decrease.
-   Distinguish percentage points from percentage change.
-   Practice: convert 3/8 to a percentage; increase 800 by 12%; decrease
    500 by 20%.
-   **You should be able to:** move confidently between fractions,
    decimals, and percentages.

## Day 5 --- Ratios, rates, and proportions

-   Understand ratios and equivalent ratios.
-   Simplify ratios.
-   Solve direct-proportion problems using unit rates.
-   Understand inverse proportion.
-   Learn the meaning of scale and rate per unit.
-   Practice: divide 840 in a 2:5 ratio; calculate a unit price; solve a
    simple speed/time problem.
-   **ML connection:** ratios appear in normalization, class
    proportions, and train/test splits.
-   **You should be able to:** set up and solve a proportion from a word
    problem.

## Day 6 --- Factors, multiples, primes, GCD, and LCM

-   Identify factors and multiples.
-   Understand prime and composite numbers.
-   Use prime factorization.
-   Calculate GCD and least common multiple (LCM).
-   Use GCD to simplify fractions and LCM to find common denominators.
-   Practice: prime-factorize 84; find GCD and LCM of 18 and 30.
-   **You should be able to:** factor integers and use GCD/LCM in
    arithmetic problems.

## Day 7 --- Exponents and powers

-   Understand base and exponent.
-   Learn exponent rules:
    -   a\^m × a\^n = a\^(m+n);
    -   a\^m / a\^n = a\^(m−n), for a ≠ 0;
    -   (a^m)^n = a\^(mn);
    -   (ab)\^n = a\^n b\^n;
    -   a\^0 = 1, for a ≠ 0;
    -   a\^(−n) = 1/a\^n.
-   Work with powers of 10 and scientific notation.
-   Practice simplifying expressions with positive, zero, and negative
    exponents.
-   **You should be able to:** simplify exponent expressions and
    interpret scientific notation.

## Day 8 --- Roots and radicals

-   Understand square roots, cube roots, and nth roots.
-   Connect roots to fractional exponents: √a = a\^(1/2), and a\^(m/n) =
    (ⁿ√a)\^m where defined.
-   Simplify radicals by extracting perfect-square factors.
-   Add and multiply like radicals where valid.
-   Understand that √(a+b) is generally not equal to √a + √b.
-   Practice: simplify √72; calculate √25 and ³√64; simplify 2√3 + 5√3.
-   **You should be able to:** simplify basic radical expressions and
    convert between roots and fractional powers.

## Day 9 --- Absolute value and distance

-   Define absolute value as distance from zero: \|x\|.
-   Understand \|x\| ≥ 0 and \|−x\| = \|x\|.
-   Learn \|ab\| = \|a\|\|b\| and \|a+b\| ≤ \|a\|+\|b\| (triangle
    inequality).
-   Solve simple absolute-value equations and inequalities.
-   Interpret \|a−b\| as distance between two points on a number line.
-   Practice: solve \|x\| = 5; solve \|x−2\| \< 3; calculate \|−7−4\|.
-   **ML connection:** absolute error and L1 distance use absolute
    values.
-   **You should be able to:** interpret absolute value geometrically
    and solve basic absolute-value inequalities.

## Day 10 --- Arithmetic review and checkpoint

-   Review fractions, signs, percentages, ratios, powers, roots, and
    absolute value.
-   Complete a mixed problem set without notes.
-   Make a one-page reference sheet of rules you keep forgetting.
-   **Checkpoint:** aim for at least 80% accuracy on 25 mixed questions.
    Revisit weak days before continuing.

# Part 2 --- Algebra and equations (Days 11--22)

**Goal:** Learn to work with unknowns and expressions. Algebra is
essential for understanding model equations, gradients, and mathematical
derivations.

## Day 11 --- Variables, constants, and algebraic expressions

-   Understand variables, constants, coefficients, terms, and
    expressions.
-   Translate simple sentences into algebra.
-   Evaluate an expression for given variable values.
-   Identify like and unlike terms.
-   Practice: evaluate 3x² − 2x + 4 at x = −2; translate "five more than
    twice x."
-   **You should be able to:** read and write basic algebraic
    expressions.

## Day 12 --- Simplifying expressions

-   Combine like terms.
-   Apply the distributive property: a(b+c) = ab+ac.
-   Remove brackets and simplify nested expressions.
-   Understand why unlike terms cannot be combined.
-   Practice: simplify 3(2x−4) + 5x − 7.
-   **You should be able to:** simplify multi-term expressions without
    changing their value.

## Day 13 --- Linear equations in one variable

-   Understand an equation as a statement that two expressions are
    equal.
-   Solve equations using inverse operations.
-   Keep both sides balanced by performing the same operation on each
    side.
-   Solve equations with brackets and fractions.
-   Practice: 3x+7=22; 2(x−3)=x+5; x/3 + 2 = 7.
-   **You should be able to:** solve a linear equation and verify the
    solution by substitution.

## Day 14 --- Rearranging formulas

-   Make a chosen variable the subject of a formula.
-   Rearrange formulas involving multiplication, division, powers, and
    roots.
-   Understand that rearranging is useful even when no numerical values
    are supplied.
-   Practice: make t the subject of d=vt; make r the subject of A=πr².
-   **ML connection:** rearranging equations helps interpret model
    parameters and derive formulas.
-   **You should be able to:** isolate a requested variable correctly.

## Day 15 --- Inequalities

-   Understand \<, \>, ≤, and ≥.
-   Solve linear inequalities.
-   Remember that multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses
    the inequality sign.
-   Represent solutions on a number line and using interval notation.
-   Practice: 3x−4\<11; −2x≥6; −1≤2x+3\<7.
-   **You should be able to:** solve and graph basic inequalities.

## Day 16 --- Coordinate plane and ordered pairs

-   Understand x- and y-axes, origin, quadrants, and ordered pairs.
-   Plot points and read coordinates.
-   Understand horizontal and vertical changes.
-   Calculate the distance between points using the Pythagorean theorem.
-   Practice: plot (−2,3), (4,−1), and find the distance between (1,2)
    and (4,6).
-   **You should be able to:** interpret points and distances in a
    two-dimensional coordinate system.

## Day 17 --- Slope and straight lines

-   Understand slope as rise/run.
-   Calculate slope between two points: m=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁).
-   Interpret positive, negative, zero, and undefined slopes.
-   Learn slope-intercept form y=mx+b and point-slope form.
-   Practice: find the line through (1,3) and (3,7); interpret its slope
    and intercept.
-   **ML connection:** linear regression fits a line or, in higher
    dimensions, a hyperplane.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate slope and write the equation of
    a line.

## Day 18 --- Systems of linear equations

-   Understand a system as multiple equations that must hold
    simultaneously.
-   Solve two-variable systems by substitution and elimination.
-   Interpret the intersection of two lines.
-   Recognize one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions.
-   Practice: solve 2x+y=7 and x−y=2.
-   **You should be able to:** solve a two-equation linear system and
    explain its graphical meaning.

## Day 19 --- Polynomials and algebraic operations

-   Understand monomials, binomials, trinomials, and polynomial degree.
-   Add, subtract, and multiply polynomials.
-   Multiply binomials using distribution.
-   Learn common identities: (a+b)², (a−b)², and (a+b)(a−b).
-   Practice expanding (x+3)(x−5) and (2x−1)².
-   **You should be able to:** expand and simplify polynomial
    expressions.

## Day 20 --- Factoring

-   Factor out the greatest common factor.
-   Factor simple trinomials.
-   Recognize difference of squares: a²−b²=(a−b)(a+b).
-   Connect factoring with solving equations.
-   Practice: factor x²−5x+6; 3x²+12x; 4x²−25.
-   **You should be able to:** factor common polynomial forms and check
    by expansion.

## Day 21 --- Quadratic equations

-   Understand standard form ax²+bx+c=0.
-   Solve by factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic
    formula.
-   Understand the discriminant b²−4ac and what it says about real
    roots.
-   Connect roots to x-intercepts of a parabola.
-   Practice: solve x²−5x+6=0 and 2x²+3x−2=0.
-   **You should be able to:** solve a quadratic using at least two
    methods and interpret its roots.

## Day 22 --- Algebra review and checkpoint

-   Complete mixed exercises on expressions, equations, inequalities,
    lines, systems, polynomials, and quadratics.
-   Explain the difference between an expression, equation, and
    function.
-   **Checkpoint:** solve 20 mixed problems with at least 80% accuracy.
    Revisit any weak topic.

# Part 3 --- Functions, graphs, and logarithms (Days 23--32)

**Goal:** Understand functions as mappings and learn the function
families that appear throughout ML.

## Day 23 --- What is a function?

-   Understand input, output, domain, and range.
-   Use function notation f(x).
-   Evaluate functions for numerical inputs.
-   Distinguish a function from an equation with multiple possible
    y-values for one x.
-   Practice: evaluate f(x)=2x²−3 at x=−1, 0, and 2.
-   **You should be able to:** explain a function as a rule mapping each
    allowed input to exactly one output.

## Day 24 --- Function graphs and transformations

-   Read graphs and identify intercepts, increasing/decreasing
    intervals, and turning points.
-   Understand vertical and horizontal shifts.
-   Understand vertical stretching/compression and reflection.
-   Sketch simple linear and quadratic functions.
-   Practice graphing y=x², y=(x−2)²+1, and y=−2x+3.
-   **You should be able to:** describe how changing a function's
    formula changes its graph.

## Day 25 --- Domain, range, and piecewise functions

-   Find domains from restrictions such as division by zero and square
    roots of negative values (over the reals).
-   Determine range for simple functions.
-   Understand piecewise-defined functions.
-   Practise evaluating a function using different rules in different
    intervals.
-   **ML connection:** activation functions and loss functions can be
    defined piecewise.
-   **You should be able to:** determine valid inputs and evaluate
    simple piecewise functions.

## Day 26 --- Function composition and inverse functions

-   Understand f(g(x)) as applying g first and then f.
-   Calculate compositions.
-   Understand one-to-one functions and why they matter for inverses.
-   Find simple inverse functions by swapping x and y and solving.
-   Practice with f(x)=2x+1 and g(x)=x².
-   **You should be able to:** compose simple functions and find an
    inverse when it exists.

## Day 27 --- Exponential functions

-   Understand functions of the form f(x)=a·b\^x.
-   Compare exponential growth and decay.
-   Learn the natural exponential function e\^x.
-   Understand the role of e in continuous growth.
-   Sketch simple exponential curves.
-   **ML connection:** exponentials appear in probability distributions
    and softmax.
-   **You should be able to:** interpret exponential growth/decay and
    evaluate basic exponential expressions.

## Day 28 --- Logarithms

-   Understand logarithms as the inverse of exponentiation: log_b(x)=y
    means b\^y=x.
-   Learn common logarithm (base 10) and natural logarithm (base e).
-   Learn log rules:
    -   log(ab)=log(a)+log(b);
    -   log(a/b)=log(a)−log(b);
    -   log(a\^k)=k log(a).
-   Understand the domain: the argument of a real logarithm must be
    positive; the base must be positive and not 1.
-   Practice converting between exponential and logarithmic form.
-   **ML connection:** log-likelihood and cross-entropy use logarithms.
-   **You should be able to:** convert, evaluate, and simplify basic
    logarithmic expressions.

## Day 29 --- Trigonometry foundations

-   Understand angles in degrees and radians.
-   Learn sine, cosine, and tangent using right triangles and the unit
    circle.
-   Memorize key unit-circle values for 0, π/6, π/4, π/3, and π/2.
-   Understand periodicity and basic sine/cosine graphs.
-   **You should be able to:** evaluate common trigonometric values and
    understand radians.

## Day 30 --- Pythagorean theorem and trigonometric identities

-   Use a²+b²=c² for right triangles.
-   Learn sin²θ+cos²θ=1.
-   Understand basic relationships between sine, cosine, and tangent.
-   Solve simple triangle and coordinate problems.
-   **You should be able to:** use the Pythagorean theorem and the
    fundamental trigonometric identity.

## Day 31 --- Sequences and series

-   Understand sequences and their terms.
-   Learn arithmetic sequences and geometric sequences.
-   Understand finite sums and sigma notation.
-   Learn the geometric-series sum formula.
-   Practise identifying a sequence rule and calculating its nth term.
-   **ML connection:** sums appear in losses, averages, and probability.
-   **You should be able to:** express a pattern as a sequence and
    calculate simple sums.

## Day 32 --- Functions and logarithms review

-   Review linear, quadratic, exponential, logarithmic, and
    trigonometric functions.
-   Sketch representative graphs from memory.
-   Solve mixed function, domain, composition, and log problems.
-   **Checkpoint:** explain why logarithms undo exponentials and why
    functions are central to mathematical modelling.

# Part 4 --- Descriptive statistics and data mathematics (Days 33--43)

**Goal:** Learn how to summarize data and quantify variation before
studying probability and inference.

## Day 33 --- Data types and frequency

-   Distinguish categorical and numerical data.
-   Distinguish discrete and continuous variables.
-   Understand frequency, relative frequency, and cumulative frequency.
-   Read frequency tables.
-   **You should be able to:** classify variables and build a frequency
    table.

## Day 34 --- Mean, median, and mode

-   Calculate arithmetic mean.
-   Find median after sorting values.
-   Find mode and understand when there may be multiple modes or no
    useful mode.
-   Compare how mean and median respond to outliers.
-   Practice with a small dataset containing an extreme value.
-   **You should be able to:** select and calculate a suitable measure
    of central tendency.

## Day 35 --- Weighted mean and percentiles

-   Calculate weighted averages.
-   Understand quartiles, deciles, and percentiles.
-   Interpret the median as the 50th percentile.
-   Understand rank-based position in a dataset.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate weighted means and interpret
    percentiles.

## Day 36 --- Range, variance, and standard deviation

-   Calculate range.
-   Understand deviations from the mean.
-   Learn population variance and sample variance.
-   Understand why squared deviations are used.
-   Calculate standard deviation as the square root of variance.
-   Distinguish population and sample denominators.
-   **ML connection:** variance measures spread and appears in
    standardization and model assumptions.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate and interpret variance and
    standard deviation for a small dataset.

## Day 37 --- Interquartile range and outliers

-   Calculate Q1, Q2, and Q3.
-   Calculate IQR=Q3−Q1.
-   Learn the 1.5×IQR rule for flagging potential outliers.
-   Understand box plots.
-   Recognize that an outlier is not automatically an error.
-   **You should be able to:** use IQR to describe spread and identify
    potential outliers.

## Day 38 --- Shape of distributions

-   Understand symmetry, skewness, and tails.
-   Compare normal, uniform, and skewed distributions visually.
-   Understand how mean and median relate under skew.
-   Learn kurtosis at a conceptual level.
-   **You should be able to:** describe a distribution's shape in plain
    language.

## Day 39 --- Covariance

-   Understand how two variables vary together.
-   Calculate covariance conceptually and for a small dataset.
-   Interpret positive, negative, and near-zero covariance.
-   Understand why covariance magnitude depends on measurement scales.
-   **You should be able to:** explain what covariance says and what it
    does not say.

## Day 40 --- Correlation

-   Understand Pearson correlation.
-   Learn that correlation is between −1 and +1.
-   Interpret positive, negative, and near-zero linear relationships.
-   Understand that correlation does not establish causation.
-   Distinguish correlation from covariance.
-   **ML connection:** correlation helps with exploratory analysis but
    does not by itself determine feature usefulness.
-   **You should be able to:** interpret a correlation coefficient
    responsibly.

## Day 41 --- Standard scores and normalization

-   Calculate z-scores: z=(x−μ)/σ.
-   Understand how many standard deviations a value is from the mean.
-   Distinguish standardization from min-max scaling.
-   Understand why scale matters for distance-based methods and
    gradient-based optimization.
-   **You should be able to:** standardize a value by hand and explain
    what the result means.

## Day 42 --- Visual summaries and statistical interpretation

-   Understand histograms, bar charts, scatter plots, box plots, and
    line plots.
-   Choose a plot based on variable type and question.
-   Identify misleading axes and distorted comparisons.
-   Practise describing a chart without claiming more than it shows.
-   **You should be able to:** choose an appropriate visual summary and
    interpret it accurately.

## Day 43 --- Descriptive statistics checkpoint

-   Calculate mean, median, variance, standard deviation, quartiles,
    IQR, covariance, and correlation for small datasets.
-   Explain how outliers affect common statistics.
-   **Checkpoint:** complete one end-to-end descriptive-statistics
    worksheet without notes.

# Part 5 --- Probability (Days 44--58)

**Goal:** Understand uncertainty, random events, conditional
probability, random variables, and common distributions.

## Day 44 --- Probability foundations

-   Understand experiment, outcome, sample space, and event.
-   Learn probability bounds: 0≤P(A)≤1.
-   Understand complements: P(Aᶜ)=1−P(A).
-   Calculate probabilities for equally likely outcomes.
-   **You should be able to:** define a sample space and calculate
    simple event probabilities.

## Day 45 --- Event operations

-   Understand union, intersection, and complement.
-   Learn mutually exclusive events.
-   Use P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B).
-   Practise with dice, cards, and simple event diagrams.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate probabilities involving unions
    and intersections.

## Day 46 --- Counting principles

-   Learn the multiplication rule for counting.
-   Understand factorials.
-   Distinguish permutations (order matters) from combinations (order
    does not matter).
-   Calculate nPr and nCr.
-   Practise small selection and arrangement problems.
-   **You should be able to:** decide whether a problem requires
    permutations or combinations.

## Day 47 --- Conditional probability

-   Understand P(A\|B): probability of A given B.
-   Learn P(A\|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B), when P(B)\>0.
-   Use two-way tables and tree diagrams.
-   Distinguish P(A\|B) from P(B\|A).
-   **You should be able to:** calculate conditional probabilities and
    explain the conditioning event.

## Day 48 --- Independence

-   Understand independent events.
-   Learn P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B) for independent events.
-   Distinguish independence from mutually exclusive events.
-   Practise checking independence using probabilities.
-   **You should be able to:** explain why independent and mutually
    exclusive are not the same idea.

## Day 49 --- Bayes' theorem

-   Derive Bayes' theorem from conditional probability.
-   Learn prior, likelihood, evidence, and posterior.
-   Solve medical-test and classification-style examples.
-   Understand base-rate effects.
-   **ML connection:** Bayesian reasoning and Naive Bayes
    classification.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate a posterior probability and
    explain each term.

## Day 50 --- Random variables

-   Understand a random variable as a numerical value assigned to an
    outcome.
-   Distinguish discrete and continuous random variables.
-   Learn probability mass functions (PMFs) and probability density
    functions (PDFs).
-   Understand that a continuous PDF's height is not itself a
    probability; probabilities are areas.
-   **You should be able to:** distinguish PMF and PDF and interpret
    each correctly.

## Day 51 --- Cumulative distribution functions

-   Understand the CDF F(x)=P(X≤x).
-   Connect the CDF to PMF/PDF.
-   Read probabilities from a CDF.
-   Understand quantiles as inverse-CDF concepts.
-   **You should be able to:** interpret a CDF and calculate simple
    cumulative probabilities.

## Day 52 --- Expected value

-   Understand expectation as a probability-weighted average.
-   Calculate expectation for discrete random variables.
-   Learn linearity of expectation.
-   Understand that E\[X+Y\]=E\[X\]+E\[Y\], even without independence.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate expected values and interpret
    them as long-run averages.

## Day 53 --- Variance of a random variable

-   Learn Var(X)=E\[(X−E\[X\])²\].
-   Use Var(X)=E\[X²\]−(E\[X\])².
-   Understand standard deviation.
-   Learn Var(aX+b)=a²Var(X).
-   Understand the role of independence in variance of sums.
-   **You should be able to:** calculate variance for a small discrete
    distribution.

## Day 54 --- Bernoulli and binomial distributions

-   Understand Bernoulli trials and binary outcomes.
-   Learn the Bernoulli distribution.
-   Understand binomial assumptions: fixed number of trials, independent
    trials, same success probability.
-   Calculate binomial probabilities and expectation/variance.
-   **ML connection:** binary labels and repeated success/failure
    events.
-   **You should be able to:** identify when a binomial model is
    appropriate.

## Day 55 --- Categorical, multinomial, and uniform distributions

-   Understand categorical outcomes with multiple classes.
-   Learn the multinomial distribution conceptually.
-   Understand discrete and continuous uniform distributions.
-   Calculate simple probabilities, means, and variances where
    appropriate.
-   **You should be able to:** choose a suitable basic distribution for
    a simple experiment.

## Day 56 --- Normal distribution

-   Understand the bell-shaped normal curve.
-   Learn mean μ and standard deviation σ.
-   Understand standard normal Z\~N(0,1).
-   Convert X to a z-score.
-   Learn the empirical 68--95--99.7 rule.
-   **ML connection:** normal assumptions appear in modelling,
    measurement noise, and initialization discussions.
-   **You should be able to:** standardize a normal value and interpret
    its relative position.

## Day 57 --- Poisson and exponential distributions

-   Understand Poisson counts over a fixed interval.
-   Learn the rate parameter λ and E\[X\]=Var(X)=λ for a Poisson
    variable.
-   Understand exponential waiting times and their relationship to
    Poisson processes.
-   Recognize when these models are plausible.
-   **You should be able to:** distinguish count models from
    waiting-time models.

## Day 58 --- Probability review and checkpoint

-   Solve mixed problems involving counting, conditional probability,
    Bayes, random variables, expectation, variance, and distributions.
-   **Checkpoint:** explain the difference between probability of an
    event and density at a point.

# Part 6 --- Statistical inference (Days 59--69)

**Goal:** Learn how to use samples to reason about populations, quantify
uncertainty, and evaluate evidence.

## Day 59 --- Population, sample, and sampling

-   Distinguish population, sample, parameter, and statistic.
-   Understand random sampling and sampling bias.
-   Learn sampling with and without replacement.
-   Understand why representative sampling matters.
-   **You should be able to:** identify sources of sampling bias in
    examples.

## Day 60 --- Sampling distributions

-   Understand that a statistic varies from sample to sample.
-   Distinguish a data distribution from a sampling distribution.
-   Understand the sampling distribution of the sample mean.
-   Learn the standard error of the mean: σ/√n when population σ is
    known.
-   **You should be able to:** explain standard error and why it usually
    decreases as sample size increases.

## Day 61 --- Central Limit Theorem

-   Understand the CLT conceptually.
-   Learn when sample means become approximately normal.
-   Understand that the CLT concerns sampling distributions, not a claim
    that raw data are always normal.
-   Explore how sample size and skew affect approximation.
-   **You should be able to:** explain the CLT accurately and identify a
    common misconception.

## Day 62 --- Confidence intervals

-   Understand point estimates and interval estimates.
-   Interpret a confidence level correctly in repeated-sampling terms.
-   Learn the basic confidence interval for a mean when assumptions are
    appropriate.
-   Understand margin of error and the role of sample size and
    variability.
-   **You should be able to:** interpret a confidence interval without
    saying there is a 95% probability that a fixed parameter lies in the
    already-computed interval.

## Day 63 --- Hypothesis testing

-   Understand null and alternative hypotheses.
-   Learn test statistic, significance level, and p-value.
-   Understand the logic of evidence against a null hypothesis.
-   Distinguish statistical significance from practical importance.
-   **You should be able to:** describe the steps of a hypothesis test.

## Day 64 --- Type I and Type II errors, power

-   Understand false positives (Type I) and false negatives (Type II).
-   Learn statistical power as the probability of detecting an effect
    when it exists under a specified alternative.
-   Understand trade-offs among significance level, sample size, effect
    size, and power.
-   **ML connection:** classification errors are related in spirit, but
    do not confuse them with every detail of hypothesis-testing errors.
-   **You should be able to:** explain the two error types using a
    concrete example.

## Day 65 --- Common statistical tests

-   Understand the purpose of a one-sample and two-sample t-test.
-   Learn paired versus independent samples.
-   Understand chi-square tests for categorical data at a conceptual

-   Learn the idea of ANOVA for comparing multiple group means.
-   Focus on assumptions and interpretation, not memorizing every
    formula.
-   **You should be able to:** select a plausible test for a basic study
    design.

## Day 66 --- Likelihood

-   Understand a likelihood function.
-   Learn why likelihood is treated as a function of parameters for

-   Compare likelihood values for candidate parameter settings.
-   **You should be able to:** explain likelihood in your own words.

## Day 67 --- Maximum likelihood estimation

## Day 68 --- Bayesian inference and MAP

-   Understand how a prior can regularize estimates.
-   **You should be able to:** describe the conceptual difference

## Day 69 --- Inference review and checkpoint

# Formula reference

This is a compact reference, not a substitute for understanding the
derivations.

## Arithmetic and algebra

-   Percentage: `percentage = (part / whole) × 100`
-   Slope: `m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)`
-   Distance in 2D: `d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)`
-   Quadratic formula: `x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a)`
-   Difference of squares: `a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)`

## Exponents and logarithms

-   `aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ`
-   `aᵐ / aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ`
-   `(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ`
-   `log(ab) = log(a) + log(b)`
-   `log(a/b) = log(a) − log(b)`
-   `log(aᵏ) = k log(a)`

## Statistics

-   Mean: `x̄ = (Σxᵢ)/n`
-   Population variance: `σ² = Σ(xᵢ−μ)²/N`
-   Sample variance: `s² = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²/(n−1)`
-   Standard deviation: square root of variance
-   z-score: `z = (x−μ)/σ`
-   Pearson correlation: covariance divided by the product of standard
    deviations

## Probability

-   Complement: `P(Aᶜ)=1−P(A)`
-   Addition rule: `P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)`
-   Conditional probability: `P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B)`
-   Independence: `P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B)`
-   Bayes: `P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B)`
-   Expected value (discrete): `E[X]=ΣxP(X=x)`
-   Variance: `Var(X)=E[X²]−(E[X])²`

## Linear algebra

-   L2 norm: `||x||₂ = √(Σxᵢ²)`
-   Dot product: `x·y = Σxᵢyᵢ = ||x||||y||cos(θ)`
-   Matrix-vector multiplication: `y=Ax`
-   Eigenvector equation: `Av=λv`
-   Rank-nullity: `rank(A)+nullity(A)=number of columns of A`

## Calculus and optimization

-   Power rule: `d(xⁿ)/dx = nxⁿ⁻¹`
-   Product rule: `(fg)'=f'g+fg'`
-   Chain rule: `(f(g(x)))'=f'(g(x))g'(x)`
-   `d(eˣ)/dx=eˣ`
-   `d(ln x)/dx=1/x`
-   Gradient descent: `θₜ₊₁=θₜ−η∇L(θₜ)`

# What to do after Day 120

Do not assume that finishing this schedule means every advanced topic is
mastered. Mathematics improves through repeated use.

As you study each ML topic, revisit the relevant mathematics: - Linear
regression: algebra, functions, vectors, matrices, least squares,
derivatives. - Logistic regression: functions, exponentials, logarithms,
probability, gradients. - Decision trees: probability, entropy,
information gain. - K-means: vectors, distances, means, optimization. -
PCA: covariance, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, SVD. - Neural networks:
matrix multiplication, derivatives, chain rule, gradients,
optimization. - Probabilistic models: conditional probability, Bayes,
distributions, likelihood, MLE. - Embeddings and retrieval: vectors,
norms, dot products, cosine similarity. - Deep learning training:
gradients, Jacobians, numerical stability, optimization.

## Important advice

-   Spend more time solving problems than watching videos.
-   Keep a notebook of mistakes and the reason each mistake happened.
-   Derive important formulas at least once; do not only memorize them.
-   Use small numbers and simple examples before moving to abstract
    notation.
-   If a topic is difficult, spend extra days on it. The day numbers are
    a sequence, not a deadline.
-   Learn the meaning of every symbol in a formula before trying to use
    it.
